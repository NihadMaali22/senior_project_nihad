Bachelor's Degree Conferral Regulations
Arab American University – Palestine
Approved: 22 September 2022
Article (1): Name and Applicability
1. These regulations shall be called the "Bachelor's Degree Conferral Regulations" at the Arab American University.
2. These regulations shall apply to all students of the University as of the date of their approval.
Article (2): Definitions
The following words and phrases used in these regulations shall have the meanings indicated:
University: The Arab American University.
University Council: The Council of the Arab American University.
Deans' Council: The Council of Deans of the Arab American University.
Faculty Council: The council of a faculty within the University.
Department Council: The council of an academic department/major.
President: The President of the University.
Dean: The Dean of a faculty within the University.
Department Chair: The head of an academic department within the University.
Instructor: A faculty member at the University.
Student: A student enrolled at the Arab American University.
Article (3): Scope
The provisions of these regulations apply to all students enrolled to obtain a bachelor's degree in all faculties of the University, with due regard to the existence of special regulations for conferring the bachelor's degree in the College of Medicine and the College of Dentistry.
ACADEMIC PLAN
Article (4): Academic Plans Committee
1. The Deans' Council shall form a committee called the Academic Plans Committee, whose task is to study the plans submitted by the deanships.
2. The Deans' Council approves academic plans or amendments thereto leading to the bachelor's degree in various faculties, based on recommendations of faculty councils and nominations from relevant academic department councils, after the Academic Plans Committee has studied them and submitted its recommendation to the University President, who then presents them to the Deans' Council for approval.
Article (5): Minimum Credit Hours
The minimum number of credit hours required to obtain a bachelor's degree in each major shall be as officially approved in the academic plans for those majors.
Article (6): Academic Plan Components
The academic plan includes the following requirements:
First: University Requirements (22 credit hours) (1):
a. Obligatory University Requirements: Taken by all students; allocated 14 credit hours.
b. Elective University Requirements: Taken by all students; allocated 8 credit hours (2).
Second: Faculty Requirements: Mandatory and elective courses (if any) studied by the student according to their academic plan.
Third: Department Requirements: Mandatory and elective courses (if any) studied by the student according to their academic plan.
Fourth: Major Requirements: Mandatory and elective courses (if any) studied by the student according to their academic plan.
Fifth: Supporting Courses: A set of mandatory courses (if any) studied by the student according to their academic plan.
Sixth: Free Elective Courses: All courses offered by the University except those prohibited (Not Allowed) according to the academic plan. A maximum of 6 credit hours is allocated.
(1) For students enrolled starting from academic year 2017/2018: Dentistry and Nursing students have 16 university credit hours (only 2 elective credit hours); Medicine students have 20 hours. For students enrolled before that, it is 24 hours.
(2) For students enrolled from academic year 2017/2018: Dentistry and Nursing students have 2 elective university credit hours. For those enrolled before, it is 9 hours.
STUDY DURATION AND ACADEMIC LOAD
Article (7): Academic Year Structure and Student Classification
1. The academic year consists of two mandatory regular semesters, each lasting sixteen weeks including the examination period, and an optional summer session (except for the College of Medicine and College of Dentistry) (3) lasting eight weeks including the examination period. These are distributed throughout the academic year according to the academic calendar approved by the Deans' Council.
2. The number of teaching hours in the summer session shall equal the number of teaching hours in any regular semester.
3. The summer session is considered an extension of the second semester for the purposes of academic warnings, GPA calculation, and academic dismissal.
4. A student enrolled for a bachelor's degree is classified into academic levels upon successfully completing the following numbers of credit hours from their plan:
Level	Credit Hours Passed	Student Level
Year 1	Less than 35	Freshman
Year 2	35–64	Sophomore
Year 3	65–94	Junior
Year 4	95–129	Senior
Year 5	130 and above	Senior

Student level in the College of Medicine and College of Dentistry is determined by the level of core courses registered in that year.
5. Credit hours are calculated on the basis that one credit hour equals 16 teaching hours. Laboratory and practical hours are evaluated per course, with one credit hour equaling two or three contact hours per week, based on faculty recommendation and Deans' Council approval.
6. The student is responsible for registering courses included in their academic plan in accordance with the academic advising and registration procedures followed by the University.

1. The minimum and maximum study duration allowed to obtain a bachelor's degree is as follows:
#	Faculty	Minimum	Maximum

2	College of Sciences	3 years	8 years
3	College of Administrative & Financial Sciences	3 years	8 years
4	College of Arts	3 years	8 years

2. Subject to the maximum study duration, the minimum credit hours a student must register per regular semester is 12, and the maximum is 19 credit hours.
3. A student may, with the Dean's approval, register fewer than the minimum specified in paragraph (2), but not less than 9 credit hours. Students in Medicine or Dentistry who must repeat a year may register any number of hours for courses to be retaken according to the special regulations.
4. A student may not register more than 10 credit hours in the summer session.
5. A student on the Honor Roll may register 21 credit hours in the semester immediately following the one in which they achieved Honor Roll status, provided that semester is a regular semester.
6. A student under academic warning may not register more than 16 credit hours in the regular semester following the one in which the warning was issued.
7. A student expected to graduate may register 3 additional credit hours beyond their normal academic load in the expected graduation semester during any regular semester.
8. Courses with zero credit hours are included in the student's academic load based on their weekly teaching hours.
9. The maximum credit hours a student may register in a regular semester does not exceed 15 credit hours while at the beginner or intermediate English language level, in addition to the English language course.
10. If a student needs fewer than 9 credit hours in their graduation semester, they may register only what is needed for graduation.
ATTENDANCE AND ABSENCE
Article (9): Attendance Requirement
All students are required to attend and maintain regular presence in all lectures, discussions, and practical hours as prescribed for each course in the academic plan.
Article (10): Absence Penalties
If a student is absent — whether excused or unexcused — from lectures, discussions, or practical hours by at least 25% of the prescribed hours for a course, the grade "Withdrawal" (W) shall be recorded in their academic record. Special regulations of the College of Medicine apply.
1. A first warning is issued to the student upon reaching 10% absence of total hours, via the University's electronic portal.
2. A second warning is issued to the student upon reaching 18% absence of total hours, via the University's electronic portal.
Article (11): Absence from Final Examination
If a student is absent from the final examination, the following procedure applies:
1. The instructor records the grade (FA) for the student in that course, which equals 0.35 points (4) and is included in the semester and cumulative GPA.
2. For the student to obtain the right to take the final examination — i.e., to receive an Incomplete (I) — they must submit an acceptable excuse within no more than one week from the removal of the excuse.
3. If the above condition is met (Article 11, Item 2), the grade is changed from (FA) to Incomplete (I), and the student may take an Incomplete examination within the first two weeks from the start of registration for the semester following the one in which the (I) was obtained (excluding the summer session). The faculty deanship shall follow up on the supplementary examination schedule.
4. Acceptable excuses are as follows:
a. Absence due to death of a relative (father, mother, siblings, spouse, grandparents, or children).
b. Detention, with proof submitted through the Student Affairs Deanship.
c. Visa renewal for students residing abroad.
d. Hospitalization due to illness, with a medical report from the hospital endorsed by the University clinic.
5. The medical committee formed by the Deans' Council is the official medical reference at the University.
6. A student holding an Incomplete grade may not register for courses until the grade is recorded and their GPA is calculated.
7. Incomplete examination results shall be sent to the Admissions and Registration Deanship within 24 hours of the end of the supplementary examinations.
8. If a student holding an Incomplete (I) grade does not sit for the supplementary examination, the grade (FA) shall be recorded (5), equaling 0.35 points, included in the semester and cumulative GPA, within one week of the end of supplementary examinations.
(4) A score of 0.00 is recorded for the grade (F) for students enrolled before academic year 2012/2013.
(5) A score of 0.00 is recorded for the grade (FA) for students enrolled before academic year 2012/2013.
EXAMINATIONS AND GRADES
Article (12): Grading System and Examinations
1. Grades are calculated and recorded for each course using letters and points, with the number of credit hours stated.
2. The final grade for each course is the sum of the final examination grade and the coursework grades.
3a. The final examination for all courses is held once at the end of the semester, in written or electronic form, covering the full course syllabus. It accounts for 40%–50% of the final course grade.
3b. For University requirements courses, only a midterm and a final examination are held, distributed as follows: 35% midterm, 15% participation, 50% final. In special cases, the relevant Dean may submit proposals to the Deans' Council.
4. Coursework for each course includes at least two written exams, of which students are notified at least one week in advance. The first exam must be held no later than the end of the sixth week of the regular semester (third week in summer), and the second exam in the eleventh or twelfth week (sixth week in summer).
5. Each semester exam accounts for 20%–30% of the total course grade, and papers are returned to students within one week of the exam in a regular semester and 72 hours in summer. Coursework may also include oral exams, reports, or research, as determined by the Department or Faculty Council, with the course description distributed in the first week of each semester.
6. Excluded from items (3, 4): seminars, research courses, practical/clinical exams, fieldwork, projects, and field training with credit hours. Department councils specify the grade distribution, approved by the Faculty Council, and announced at the start of the semester.
7. All exam papers, reports, and research are returned to students after grading. Final exam papers are kept at the faculty deanship for two semesters, then disposed of by the Dean.
Article (13): Makeup for Missed Semester Exams
Any student who misses an announced semester exam with an acceptable excuse (per Article 11, Item 4) or any other excuse accepted by the instructor and Dean must submit the excuse to the instructor within three days of its removal. If accepted, the instructor must administer a makeup exam. If not accepted, a zero (0) is recorded for that exam and calculated in the final grade.
Article (14): Multi-Section Course Coordination
1. The department chair shall assign one of the instructors of multi-section courses to coordinate among them regarding the course outline, prescribed books, unified examinations, and grading rubrics.
2. The instructor shall inform students at the beginning of each semester about the assessment method for the course.
3. The instructor is responsible for grading exam papers, reviewing grades, and entering them correctly and finally into the electronic system (My Portal), and submitting them to the relevant academic department within 48 hours of the final exam for single-section courses and 72 hours for multi-section courses.
4. Final grade sheets for each course, with details in percentage, letter grade, and points, are submitted by the instructor to the department chair for review, who forwards them to the faculty Dean within 24 hours for Faculty Council approval. They are then sent to the Admissions and Registration Deanship for announcement — it being the sole authority for this — and results may not be announced from any other party.
GRADE APPEALS
Article (15): Grade Appeal Process
1. Appeals are divided into two types:
First Appeal: A student may submit an initial appeal for the final exam grade within one week of the announcement of final results, using a designated form, per the following procedure:
a. The appeal is submitted to the Admissions and Registration Deanship after paying the fee, then forwarded to the relevant faculty deanship.
b. The paper is checked for addition errors and any unanswered questions.
c. The instructor signs the appeal result, which is then endorsed by the Dean and returned to Admissions and Registration.
d. If the instructor is unavailable to review the paper, a committee of three faculty members (department chair + two faculty) is formed to review it.
e. The first appeal fee is refunded if the grade is changed.
Second Appeal: A student may submit a second appeal within one week of the first appeal result, per the following procedure:
a. The appeal is submitted to the Admissions and Registration Deanship after paying the fee, then forwarded to the relevant faculty deanship.
b. A committee of three faculty members (excluding the course instructor) — comprising the department chair and two faculty members — re-grades the exam without being bound by the previous grading.
c. The committee submits its report to the faculty Dean for endorsement, then the result is sent to Admissions and Registration.
d. The second appeal grade is final.
e. Fees for both the first and second appeals are refunded if the grade is changed.
2. In all cases, the faculty must return the appeal result to the Admissions and Registration Deanship within three days from the start of the following semester.
3. There is no right of appeal for makeup examination grades in the College of Medicine and College of Dentistry.
GPA AND GRADES
Article (16): Grading Scale and GPA
1. The minimum passing grade for a course is (D).
2. Courses with grades (I, W, WV, TR, NP, P) are not included in GPA calculations, nor are their credit hours.
3. Cumulative GPA (Grade Point Average): The average of all courses studied by the student at the University under their academic plan. Calculated by multiplying each course's grade points by its credit hours, summing the products, and dividing by the total required credit hours in the student's plan.
4. Semester GPA (Semester Point Average): The average of all courses taken by the student in that semester (passed or failed). Calculated the same way as the GPA.
5. GPAs are calculated to the nearest two decimal places.
6. Course grades are recorded in letters with the corresponding points:
Grade	Points	Grade	Points
A	4.00	C	2.00
A-	3.67	C-	1.67
B+	3.33	D+	1.33
B	3.00	D	1.00
B-	2.67	F	0.35 (6)
C+	2.33	FA	0.35 (7)

7. Grade descriptions for cumulative and semester GPA:
GPA Range	Arabic Description	English Description
3.67 – 4.00	ممتاز	Excellent
3.00 – 3.66	جيد جدا	Very Good
2.33 – 2.99	جيد	Good
2.00 – 2.32	متوسط	Fair
1.67 – 1.99	مقبول	Satisfactory (8)
Below 1.67	مقصر	Dissatisfactory

8. The minimum cumulative GPA required for graduation is 1.67, and 2.00 or above for Computer Systems Engineering, Electrical Engineering and Renewable Energy, Medical Equipment Engineering, Civil Engineering, Architectural Engineering, Mechatronics Engineering (9), and Medicine.
(6) A score of 0.00 is recorded for grade (F) for students enrolled before academic year 2012/2013.
(7) A score of 0.00 is recorded for grade (FA) for students enrolled before academic year 2012/2013.
(8) For students enrolled in 2004/2005 and earlier, this tier is described as 'Fair' (متوسط).
(9) Applies to students enrolled in these majors starting from the first semester of academic year 2019/2020.
Article (17): Grade Symbols
1. The symbol (FA) denotes a student's failure due to absence from the final exam (Fail of Absence).
2. The following symbols denote courses without a recorded letter grade:
Symbol	Explanation
I (Incomplete)	Given to a student who was absent from the final examination with an acceptable excuse, per the procedure in Article (11).
W (Withdrawal)	Given when a student withdraws during the designated late-withdrawal period, which ends before the final examinations (announced in the academic calendar).
P (Pass)	Given to a student who passed courses with no grades, points, or credit hours.
NP (No Pass)	Given to a student who failed courses with no grades, points, or credit hours.
TR (Transferred Course)	Given for courses transferred/credited from other universities or colleges; not included in the GPA.
WV (Waived Course)	Given for courses from which the student was exempted during their time at the University.

COURSE REPETITION
Article (18): Rules for Repeating Courses
1. A student may not repeat a passed course unless their grade is (C) or below.
2. If a student repeats a course, all grades obtained are recorded in their academic record and transcript.
3. In all cases of repetition, the higher grade is included in the GPA, and only the higher grade is counted for graduation requirements per Article (27), with due regard to special regulations for Medicine and Dentistry students.
4. When a student repeats a course, its credit hours count toward the total required for graduation only once.
5. A student may take an alternative elective course to replace a failed elective, and similarly for a free elective. The failed course grade is not counted after passing the replacement, even if the required elective or free hours have not been completed.
6. A student who received a course exemption (WV) or had a course credited from prior study (TR) may not take that course at the University.
ACADEMIC WARNING AND DISMISSAL
Article (19): Academic Warning and Dismissal Rules
1. A student is placed on academic warning if their GPA falls below 1.67 (or 2.00 for Computer Systems Engineering, Electrical Engineering and Renewable Energy, Medical Equipment Engineering, Civil Engineering, Architectural Engineering, Mechatronics Engineering (11), and Medicine) (10) in any regular semester except the enrollment semester; summer sessions are not counted for warning or dismissal. The student must raise their GPA to 1.67 (or 2.00 (11)) or above. The Admissions and Registration Deanship notifies the student by appropriate means.
2. A student is dismissed from their major upon receiving three consecutive academic warnings due to their GPA falling below 1.67 (or 2.00 (11) for the relevant engineering and medicine majors). A dismissed student may transfer to another major subject to that major's conditions; in this case, paragraph (1) of this article applies.
3. A student is dismissed from their major upon receiving two consecutive academic warnings due to their GPA falling below 1.00. A dismissed student may transfer to another major subject to that major's conditions; paragraph (1) applies.
4. A student is dismissed from their major if they fail a mandatory major course three times, regardless of GPA or credit hours passed.
5. If a student fails a mandatory major course three times, they are given one opportunity to retake the final exam at the third failure (supplementary exam) within one week of the end of official final exams.
6a. Bachelor's students dismissed from their major due to reasons in paragraphs (2) and (3) may be admitted to remedial study. A student who transfers to a new major after remedial study may not, if dismissed from the new major, register for remedial study again. However, a student who transfers without using remedial study may benefit from remedial study if dismissed from the new major.
6b. A student admitted to remedial study is given the opportunity to raise their GPA to the required minimum in the same major to return as a regular student. The maximum study duration per Article (8) applies.
6c. The academic load for a student in remedial study is a maximum of 16 credit hours per semester for the first and second semesters.
6d. Upon admission to remedial study, one semester is deducted from the maximum graduation duration for each regular semester spent in remedial study.
6e. Tuition fees in remedial study are 50% higher than regular fees.
7. A student in remedial study may transfer to another major per the conditions of the intended major.
8a. The time limit for remedial study is a maximum of two regular semesters to raise the GPA to the required minimum; otherwise, the student is dismissed from the major.
8b. A student who has benefited from remedial study and raised their GPA may benefit again from remedial study if they remain in the same major and are dismissed again.
9a. When a student transfers from one major to another, an asterisk (*) is marked against courses studied in the old major that are not part of the new major's plan, with a note on the transcript stating that the (*) means the course is not counted in the semester or cumulative GPA.
9b. Upon acceptance into the new major, one semester is deducted from the maximum graduation duration for every 15 credit hours credited from the old major.
10. A student is dismissed from the University if they cannot join any other major after dismissal from their current one, or if dismissed from a new major after being dismissed from their original one.
11. A dismissed student may not return to the major from which they were dismissed.
12. A dismissed student (from a major or the University) may re-enroll as a new student in any University major per the admissions policy for that academic year.
13a. A student dismissed from the University may apply for re-enrollment — no sooner than one regular semester and no later than two regular semesters after dismissal — in a major other than the one from which they were dismissed, regardless of the number of previous transfers. Any dismissal after that is final.
13b. Upon approval of re-enrollment, an asterisk (*) is marked against courses from the old major not included in the new plan, with the same note as 9a.
13c. Upon approval, one semester is deducted from the maximum graduation duration for every 15 credit hours credited from the old major.
13d. The period of absence due to academic dismissal is not counted toward the permitted deferral period or maximum study duration.
14. A dismissed student is prohibited from registering any course from the major they were dismissed from as a free elective.
(10) Minimum GPA for academic warning and dismissal for students enrolled in academic year 2003/2004 and earlier is 1.50.
(11) Applies to students enrolled in these majors starting from the first semester of academic year 2019/2020.
Article (20): Exemption from Academic Dismissal After Remedial Study
1. Notwithstanding Article (19):
a. If the remaining credit hours to complete graduation requirements do not exceed 30% of the total plan credit hours, the student may continue studying to remove the academic warning (within the maximum study duration per Article (8)) with doubled credit hour fees.
b. If the student has completed all plan requirements but cannot graduate due to not meeting the graduation GPA requirement (Article 16, Item 8), they may continue studying to raise their GPA and graduate (within the maximum study duration per Article (8)) with doubled credit hour fees; otherwise they are permanently dismissed.
2. In all cases, the student must register at least 9 credit hours per semester to remove the academic warning, except for expected-to-graduate students, who are subject to Article (8) provisions.
COURSE WITHDRAWAL AND ADDITION
Article (21): Course Withdrawal and Addition
1. Students may withdraw from registered courses and add new ones during the add/drop period announced in the academic calendar at the beginning of each semester. Courses dropped during this period are not recorded in the academic record.
2. Subject to paragraph (1), a student may withdraw from one or more courses no later than the end of week fourteen. In this case, the course appears in the academic record with a (W) notation and is not counted toward passed or failed hours or graduation requirements.
3. If a student withdraws from all registered courses in a semester, their study in that semester is considered deferred. The student must submit the withdrawal request no later than the late withdrawal (W) deadline for that semester. All courses are marked (Withdrawal), the semester is counted toward the permitted deferral period per Article (22, paragraph 1), and it is recorded in the academic record.
STUDY DEFERRAL, INTERRUPTION, AND WITHDRAWAL FROM UNIVERSITY
Article (22): Deferral and Interruption
1. A student may defer their studies at the University for a total of no more than four regular semesters, whether consecutive or non-consecutive, recorded in the academic record.
2. New or transfer students may not defer until completing at least one semester at the University; they may withdraw from all registered courses per Article (21, item 3).
3. Deferral requests for unregistered students must be submitted within one month of the start of the semester.
4. If a regular or first/second semester begins and the student is neither registered nor on approved deferral, and the legal deferral period has lapsed, they are considered to have interrupted their studies (equivalent to deferral), counted from the permitted deferral period, recorded in the academic record, and a financial penalty set by the Deans' Council is imposed upon their return.
5. Deferral or interruption periods are not counted toward the maximum graduation years.
6. If a student exceeds the approved deferral period, they are treated as in paragraph (4).
7. A student who exceeds the maximum permitted deferral (four regular semesters) may not return to study except as a new student per University regulations.
8. A returning deferred or interrupted student resumes the academic plan they were on, or as determined by the department council of the student's major.
9. Detention periods are not counted toward the permitted deferral semesters or maximum graduation years.
Article (23): Withdrawal from the University
1. A student wishing to withdraw from the University must submit a request on the designated form to the Admissions and Registration Deanship. A (Withdrawn from University) notation is recorded in their file, their seat is forfeited, and the Admissions Dean notifies relevant internal and external parties.
2. A student wishing to re-enroll under their previous student number after withdrawal — within no more than four regular semesters and not exceeding the maximum permitted deferral — must submit a re-enrollment request on the designated form. If accepted in their major, their full academic record is retained and they must complete graduation requirements per the plan in effect upon return. Previous study time is counted toward the maximum graduation years. If accepted in another department, Article (26) applies.
TRANSFER AND CREDIT FROM OTHER INSTITUTIONS
Article (24): Transfer from Other Educational Institutions
Transfer applications are processed as follows:
1. Transfer applications must meet the following conditions:
a. The student must meet the University's admission requirements per the admissions policy for that year.
b. The student must be transferring from an educational institution (university, institute, or college) recognized by the Palestinian Ministry of Higher Education, and must not have completed their degree there.
c. The student must not have been dismissed for any reason (academic, behavioral, or otherwise) from the institution they are transferring from.
d. The student must present a recent transcript from the institution they are transferring from, not older than two months.
2a. The Admissions and Registration Deanship directly credits hours studied by the student at the sending institution based on the academic plan, if the course was previously credited from that same institution. New courses are credited after approval by the relevant department chair based on the course description, with a maximum of 75 credit hours for Medicine and Dentistry, 70 for engineering majors, and 60 for all other majors.
2b. Courses in which the student earned at least 70% or grade (C) or 'Good' are credited for all majors (12).
2c. Courses with at least 60% are credited for bridging students enrolled from academic year 2019/2020 onwards, in accordance with Palestinian Ministry of Higher Education regulations.
2d. The transfer student's GPA must be at least 60% or 1.67 and non-warned, except for Dentistry students and students in Computer Systems Engineering, Electrical Engineering, Medical Equipment Engineering, Civil Engineering, Architectural Engineering, and Mechatronics (11), where the minimum is 70% or 2.00 and non-warned; and Medicine, where the minimum is 75% or 2.50 and non-warned.
2e. No more than three years must have passed since the last course studied at the sending institution.
3. If a course equivalence is approved for a course studied at a recognized institution, the grade (TR) is recorded for the equivalent course and does not affect the cumulative GPA.
4. One semester is deducted from the maximum graduation years for every 15 credit hours credited from outside the University.
(12) For students who transferred from the beginning of the second semester of academic year 2018/2019.
Article (25): Crediting Hours from Outside the University for Enrolled Students
A regular student (not in a remedial study program) may study courses at another university under the following conditions:
1. The host university must be recognized by the Palestinian Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research.
2. Approval from the Deans' Council must be obtained.
3. A student may not study at another university in their first semester at the University.
4. A student may not study at another university in their final semester (the semester in which they are expected to graduate).
5. A student may study a total of one regular semester plus one summer session at another university (maximum).
6. Grades from courses studied outside the University are not included in the GPA and are recorded as (TR).
7. Courses meeting the criteria in Article (24, paragraph 2b) are credited.
INTERNAL MAJOR TRANSFER
Article (26): Transfer from One Major to Another within the University
1. Transfer requests from one major to another must be submitted to the Admissions and Registration Deanship at the beginning of the first or second semester, no later than the end of the add/drop period. Requests are processed per the admissions policy, subject to the following:
a. The student must not have been previously dismissed from the major they wish to transfer into.
b. The student's high school GPA must meet the minimum accepted for the intended major per the admissions policy for that semester, consistent with Palestinian Ministry of Higher Education regulations.
2. Upon transfer, courses studied in the old major that are part of the new major's plan are credited, and their grades are included in the cumulative GPA.
3. Upon transfer, an asterisk (*) is marked against courses studied in the old major that are not part of the new plan, with a note that (*) means the course is not counted due to major transfer.
4. Upon acceptance into the new major, one semester is deducted from the maximum graduation years for every 15 credit hours credited from the old major.
5. A student is permitted to transfer between majors within the University a maximum of two times.
BACHELOR'S DEGREE REQUIREMENTS
Article (27): Requirements for Conferral of the Bachelor's Degree
The bachelor's degree is conferred upon fulfillment of the following requirements:
1. The student must pass all courses in the academic plan prescribed for the bachelor's degree in their major and obtain a cumulative GPA of not less than 1.67 (13) in all courses studied within their plan that are counted in the cumulative GPA.
2. The maximum graduation years per Article (8, paragraph 1) must not be exceeded.
3. All tuition installments, fees, and any outstanding financial obligations to the University must be settled.
(13) Graduation GPA for Computer Systems Engineering and Electrical Engineering students is 2.00 for students enrolled from the first semester 2019/2020, and 2.00 for Medicine students.
HONOR ROLL
Article (28): Honor Roll Criteria
1. A student achieves Honor Roll status if they are registered for at least 15 credit hours in the semester (excluding P-graded courses), pass all of them, achieve a semester GPA of 3.67 or above, earn no grade below (C+) in any course taken that semester, and no active disciplinary penalty is in force at the time of GPA calculation.
2. A student is placed on the President's Honor Roll if they achieve Honor Roll status with a semester GPA of 4.00.
3. A student is placed on the Dean's Honor Roll if they achieve Honor Roll status with a semester GPA of 3.67 or above.
Article (29): Honor Roll Rewards
1. A student on the President's Honor Roll receives a tuition discount or financial reward, the value of which is determined by the Deans' Council.
2. A student on the Dean's Honor Roll — if ranked first in their major or with the highest GPA after the President's Honor Roll student — receives a tuition discount or financial reward determined by the Deans' Council, subject to:
a. If more than one student qualifies for the honor reward, each receives the same reward.
b. Three honor rewards are allocated per major if only one major exists in the faculty, subject to item (a).
Article (30): Recording and Certificates
1. Semesters in which a student appeared on Honor Roll lists are recorded on their transcript.
2. A student who achieves Honor Roll status receives an appreciation certificate from their faculty after the announcement of results.
SUBSTITUTE AND CONCURRENT COURSES
Article (31): Substitute Course
1. If a student's graduation depends on a single course that is not offered in their graduation semester, or is offered but conflicts with another course (or a similar circumstance), their faculty Dean — after consulting the department — may approve an alternative course at the same level or above, without requiring completion of the prerequisite for the substitute course. The Admissions Dean is notified in writing. If the student does not graduate, they are not required to retake the original course.
2. If a student's graduation depends on passing a course they have already attempted three or more times without success, the faculty Dean — upon recommendation of the department chair — may allow a substitute course in the graduation semester, provided the conditions of item (1) are met.
Article (32): Concurrent Enrollment in a Course and Its Prerequisite
1. A student who failed a prerequisite may register both the prerequisite and the course simultaneously, except students in Computer Systems Engineering, Electrical Engineering and Renewable Energy, Medical Equipment Engineering, Civil Engineering, Architectural Engineering, and Mechatronics Engineering (14).
2. If a student fails the first concurrent course in a chain, they may not register the course following the second course concurrently.
3. A student expected to graduate in a regular or summer semester may register courses along with their prerequisites in that semester, provided the chain does not exceed one level of prerequisite.
(14) Applies to all students in these majors starting from academic year 2019/2020.
GENERAL PROVISIONS
Article (33): Limitation on New Enrollment
A person for whom more than three years have passed since obtaining their high school diploma may not enroll as a new student in bachelor's programs offered by the University. Exceptional cases are referred to the University President for an appropriate decision. Bridging students are exempt from this provision.
Article (34): Responsibility for Academic Progress
The student's department, faculty, and the Admissions and Registration Deanship are jointly responsible for following up on the student's academic progress and verifying that graduation requirements are met.
Article (35): Prohibition on Re-enrollment After Completing Requirements
A student who has completed the requirements for the bachelor's degree is not permitted to study or re-study any course at the University for the purpose of raising their cumulative GPA. The portal is closed to prevent registration. Community service hours completion is not considered for this purpose.
Article (36): English Placement Exam upon Re-enrollment
A student who re-enrolls as a new student may retake the English language placement exam or have their previous placement exam result credited, provided no more than three years have passed since the exam was administered.
Article (37): New Student Number upon Re-enrollment
A student who re-enrolls at the University after having their old registration number cancelled may re-enroll with a new registration number under the prevailing admission conditions. They may not, under any circumstances, revert to their old number.
Article (38): Visiting Student Enrollment
Subject to Article (33), a student may enroll at the University to study one or more courses for one or several semesters as a visiting student, under the following procedure:
1. Provide a written letter from their home university approving the study of specific courses at this University, if the visiting student is regularly enrolled at another university.
2. Submit a visiting student enrollment application.
3. Pay registration fees like any regular student (including a visiting student ID card, health insurance, and a personal account on the academic electronic portal).
4. Pay credit hour fees per the approved rates for the relevant faculty.
5. Receive a transcript of courses studied during their semesters at the University.
Article (39): Re-enrollment for a Second Major
A student who has obtained a bachelor's degree from the University may not re-enroll to obtain another major at the same degree level without approval from the Deans' Council, and no courses from the previous major are credited.
Article (40): Students Remaining Only Practical Training
A student for whom only practical training remains after the current semester is treated as an expected-to-graduate student with regard to academic load per Article (8, item 7), substitute courses per Article (31), and concurrent registration with prerequisites per Article (32).
Article (41): Financial Clearance
A student must obtain financial clearance from the University upon completing graduation procedures or upon withdrawing from the University.
Article (42): Awareness of Regulations
These regulations are published on the University website. No student may claim ignorance of these regulations or of announcements issued by the University or posted on its bulletin boards regarding these regulations.
Article (43): Graduation Certificate Date
The bachelor's degree certificate shall bear the date on which it was earned.
Article (44): Dispute Resolution
The Deans' Council shall resolve any problems that may arise from the application of these regulations.
Article (45): Responsibility for Implementation
The University President, Faculty Deans, and the Dean of Admissions and Registration are tasked with implementing the provisions of these regulations.
Article (46): Repeal of Prior Regulations
These regulations repeal any previous regulations on the subject.
